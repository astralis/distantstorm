The Hallucination of Tyranny: Why a Privileged Generation Dreams of Civil War

#Gnosticism #Ingratitude #Filial Piety #Ideological Possession #False Reality

“The ideology that teaches you to call your home "hell" and your parents "oppressors" is not liberation; it is poison. It blinds you to the good so that you will burn it down. The only cure for the revolutionary mind is the humility of gratitude.”

We are witnessing a terrifying phenomenon in the West: the rise of a generation that has been catechized to hate its own existence. We see young men and women, clothed in the prosperity their ancestors earned, standing in the university square and chanting for the destruction of the very civilization that allows them to speak.

The young man’s lament is a textbook case of what we might call "Civic Gnosticism." It is a form of spiritual possession where the sufferer believes that the created world—the world of law, institutions, property, and history—is fundamentally evil, a prison created by "bad guys."

The Lens of Hatred

Notice the inversion of reality. The young man admits his life is "privileged." He admits his parents succeeded through "hard work" and "legal" means. In a sane world, this would be a cause for gratitude. It would be proof that the system, while flawed, offers a pathway to dignity.

But he has been given a pair of ideological spectacles that invert his vision. Through these lenses, his parents' success is not a virtue; it is a sign of complicity with a Nazi regime. The order that keeps the electricity running and the grocery stores full is not a blessing; it is "corruption."

This is the "inhuman humanism" I have warned against. It is an ideology that loves "Justice" in the abstract—so much so that it is willing to murder the concrete reality of the present to achieve it. When a man convinces himself that his country is Nazi Germany, he grants himself a total license. If you are fighting Nazis, you can do anything. You can burn, you can loot, you can call for "civil war." The lie justifies the violence.

The Rejection of the Father

At the heart of this despair is a sin against the Fourth Commandment: Honor thy father and thy mother.

This young man’s parents are the builders. They accepted the reality of the world, they labored within the law, and they built a home. The son, possessed by the spirit of revolution, looks at this home and sees a crime scene.

This is the great Marxist trick: to sever the bond between the generations. To tell the child that his father is not a protector, but an oppressor (or at least a dupe of the oppressors). Once that bond is broken, the child is lost. He has no past, no anchor, and no gratitude. He becomes a drift-log in the current of radical politics, desperate for a new "family" which the State or the Revolutionary Party is happy to provide.

The False Garden

The user suggests that a "civil war" might be needed to "take back our country." But take it back to where?

The "garden" this young man dreams of—the utopia that comes after the "drastic measures"—is a graveyard. It is the same garden that was promised in 1917 in Russia and 1789 in France. It is the belief that if we kill the "bad guys" (the rich, the successful, the dissenters), human nature will suddenly change.

But human nature does not change. When you tear down the "bourgeois" structures of property and law—the very structures keeping this young man from living in his car—you do not get equity. You get the raw rule of force. You get a government that really can "kill citizens without repercussions," because there is no law left to stop it.

The Cure is Reality

This young man is "ideologically possessed." He is trapped in a mental prison where Good is Evil and Evil is Good.

The only key that can unlock this prison is Gratitude. He must be challenged to look at the concrete reality of his life. Who paid for that tuition? Who built that roof? Who paved the road he drives on? These were not "Nazis." These were men and women cooperating with the order of creation.

He must be told the hard truth: The world is not broken because of "them." It is broken because of the very spirit of rebellion he is nurturing in his own heart. He wants to save the world? Let him start by taking off the ideological glasses, thanking his parents, and going to work—not to burn the world, but to serve it.
