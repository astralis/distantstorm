The Asymmetry of the Soul: Why Truth is Not a Balancing Act

#Yin and Yang#Privatio Boni#Moral Realism#Manichaeism#The Hierarchy of Being

“The Light does not dance with the Darkness -- it scatters it. We do not seek a balance between justice and cruelty. We seek the victory of the Good. A healthy body is not a balance of sickness and health -- it is whole.”

In our attempt to make sense of a fractured world, the modern mind often seeks comfort in the concept of balance. We look at the great conflicts of existence -- fate versus free will, matter versus spirit, good versus evil -- and we try to resolve them by saying that it is a bit of both. It is Yin and Yang.

This is the great temptation of Dualism. It is the idea that the universe is a closed system composed of necessary opposites. Night needs Day. Chaos needs Order. Cruelty needs Compassion.

But this is not the Christian vision. If we apply this balance to our current political crisis, we will fall into a dangerous lethargy.

The Lie of Necessary Evil

If we accept the Yin and Yang view of society, then the cruelty the previous commenter noted is simply the necessary shadow of compassion. The determinism of material poverty is just the flip side of the freedom of the wealthy. Under this view, we should not try to eradicate injustice, but merely balance it.

This is a philosophy of resignation.

The Thomistic tradition teaches us the concept of privatio boni -- the privation of the good. Evil, injustice, and suffering are not substances that exist in their own right. They are cracks in the foundation. They are wounds in the body.

A healthy body is not a balance between sickness and health. Sickness is an invader. We do not want a Yin and Yang of cancer and healthy cells. We want the total health of the organism. Similarly, we do not want a balance between the material determinism that crushes the poor and the spiritual freedom of the saint. We want the spirit to fully inhabit and redeem the material world.

The Hierarchy, Not the Circle

The user asked if the tension between our material circumstances (biology, economics) and our free will is a form of Yin and Yang.

It is not. In the Yin and Yang, the two halves are equal and cyclical. One turns into the other.

In the Christian anthropology, the relationship is hierarchical. The body is good. The spirit is good. But the spirit is meant to be the governor of the body.

When a man is ruled by his material circumstances -- when he steals because he is hungry, or hates because he was taught to hate -- he is living upside down. He has allowed the servant to become the master. He is not balancing his nature. He is disintegrating.

True freedom is when the spirit assumes its rightful place as the captain of the ship. The ship is necessary, yes. But the ship does not steer the captain.

Why This Matters for Politics

If we believe in balance, we will tolerate evils in our society as part of the cycle. We will say that there must always be some poverty to balance the wealth, or that there must be some corruption to balance the order.

But if we believe in the Victory of the Good, we look at the world differently. We see every act of cruelty not as a necessary shadow, but as an error to be corrected. We see every instance where a human person is reduced to a product of material circumstances as a theological offense.

We are not called to be balanced. We are called to be whole. The light does not compromise with the darkness. It casts it out. In the social order, this means we must never accept the deterministic excuse. We must insist that every human person, no matter their material condition, is capable of the infinite, and that society exists to help them reach it -- not to keep them in a balanced state of mediocrity.
